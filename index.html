01

FYLP · Leadership

My first year at Laurier

Finding my voice in residence

What was it that you did?

I served as Vice President of Marketing & Engagement on the First Year Leadership Program council. I helped communicate our activities and encourage students to get involved. It took regular teamwork, planning, and creative effort alongside my classes.

When did you do it?

During my first year at Wilfrid Laurier University, while I lived in Grand River Hall.

What was good about it?

The council gave me opportunities to meet people and contribute through my interest in design. It helped me practise turning an idea into something other students could understand and connect with. Those communication skills support my goal of becoming a thoughtful UX designer.

What was your favourite memory of it?

One memory that stands out is receiving recognition for my involvement. Having my contributions acknowledged made the time I had put into the council feel meaningful.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Balancing council responsibilities with coursework was challenging. I had to learn to manage my time and keep contributing even when several deadlines came together.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. The experience helped me grow, although balancing the commitments took effort.

02

Art Hawks · Design

My first year at Laurier

Making creativity visible

What was it that you did?

I contributed to the Art Hawks council through marketing and creative work, including designs for social media. I put time into making posts that represented our activities and helped students notice what we were doing.

When did you do it?

During my first year at Laurier, through my involvement with the Art Hawks council.

What was good about it?

It gave me a reason to keep creating outside of class. I could practise visual communication for a real student audience and think about how colour, layout, and wording work together. That experience connects directly to my interest in graphic design and UX.

What was your favourite memory of it?

The work I created for our posts is what I remember most. Those pieces hold memories of my time on the council, which is why preserving them matters to me.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

It can be difficult when the effort behind a design is less visible than the final image. I learned that creative work also needs clear communication about its purpose and the time it takes.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. I valued the creative opportunities and the memories connected to my work.

03

Campus ambassador · Communication

Fall 2026

Welcoming the next chapter

What was it that you did?

I worked as a Brantford Recruitment Ambassador, helping welcome prospective students and families. I prepared campus tour information, practised my delivery, and used personal stories to explain student life. I also participated in recruitment events.

When did you do it?

Fall 2026, including tour preparation and recruitment events in September and October.

What was good about it?

The role pushed me to practise English and become more comfortable speaking to new people. It also reminded me to consider what visitors need to know, rather than only what I want to say. Listening and explaining clearly are skills I can carry into UX research and future work.

What was your favourite memory of it?

A memorable part of preparing my tour was bringing in my own stories about campus, including playing music in the Research and Academic Centre. Sharing something personal helped the tour feel more like a conversation.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

I found it challenging to remember every required detail while speaking confidently. Feedback on my reverse tour showed me that I needed to project my voice and describe some services more clearly. Improving takes repeated practice.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. I enjoy connecting with visitors, and I am still building confidence in the role.

04

Piano & guitar · Creative practice

During my time at Laurier

A little room for music

What was it that you did?

I spent time playing piano and guitar on campus. Keeping music in my routine meant returning to practise, even when studying and other responsibilities made my schedule busy.

When did you do it?

During my time at Laurier, including practice sessions in Room 004 of the Research and Academic Centre.

What was good about it?

Music gave me a creative outlet alongside design and schoolwork. It also offered a way to share a part of my life with my family in Vietnam. Making time for something I enjoy supports my goal of having a balanced life with strong family connections.

What was your favourite memory of it?

My favourite memory is recording videos of myself playing and sending them to my parents in Vietnam. I also love how the room’s acoustics make playing and singing feel special.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Practising in a shared campus space can feel awkward when someone walks in. Finding time to practise consistently also takes planning around classes and work.

Give your experience a letter grade

A — Couldn’t be better. Being able to enjoy music and share it with my parents makes this experience especially meaningful to me.

05

StuDock · Making & UX

October–November 2026

Turning cardboard into an idea

What was it that you did?

I helped develop StuDock, a desk organizer made from recycled cardboard for our Group Maker Project. I proposed the name and original organizer idea, shared sketches with the group, built and trimmed the prototype, measured its parts, and helped format our presentation.

When did you do it?

October–November 2026, during the project briefing and prototype development stages.

What was good about it?

The project let me connect design ideas to a physical object. We considered how shallow compartments, removable dividers, large labels, and contrasting colours might make supplies easier to find and reach. It helped me practise considering users while working with everyday materials.

What was your favourite memory of it?

A highlight was turning the original sketch into a cardboard prototype that we could use for usability testing. Having an object to handle made the idea much easier to discuss with my group.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Measuring, cutting, and adjusting cardboard required patience. A detail that looked simple in a sketch could take several changes to work in the prototype. Coordinating the product and presentation also took consistent effort.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. I liked developing something useful, even though the small adjustments could be time-consuming.
