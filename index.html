back.

In Assignment 1, I imagined what my life might look like five years from now. This time, I’m looking back at things I’ve already put time and effort into. From campus life to creative projects, these experiences have helped me learn more about myself and what I want to do next.

01

Residence · Getting involved

My first year at Laurier

Finding my place on campus

What was it that you did?

During my first year, I got involved in the First Year Leadership Program (FYLP) while living in Grand River Hall. Being part of the council meant working with other students, helping with activities, and finding ways to get people involved in campus life.

When did you do it?

During my first year at Wilfrid Laurier University, while I was living in Grand River Hall.

What was good about it?

It gave me a chance to meet people outside my classes and feel more connected to campus. I also got to practise sharing ideas and working with a team. As an international student, having a group to contribute to helped me feel like I belonged.

What was your favourite memory of it?

Receiving recognition for my involvement was a really nice moment. It made me feel that the time and effort I had put in mattered. Looking back, I’m also glad I pushed myself to get involved instead of staying in my comfort zone.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Keeping up with council responsibilities and coursework wasn’t always easy. When deadlines came together, everything could feel like a lot. I had to learn how to plan my time and be honest about how much I could take on.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. It took some adjusting, but I’m glad I gave myself the chance to be part of campus life.

02

Art Hawks · Marketing & Design

September 2026–May 2027

Creating, connecting, and free food

What was it that you did?

I volunteered as Vice President of Marketing for the Art Hawks Council within FYLP. I designed promotional materials, managed our social media, and helped promote campus events for incoming and current first-year students. Basically, I helped get the word out and encouraged people to join in!

When did you do it?

From September 2026 to May 2027, during my first year at Laurier.

What was good about it?

I got more out of it than just meeting new people. I built friendships and improved my teamwork, communication, time management, and design skills. It was also a chance to use my creativity outside of assignments and make something that real people would see and interact with. As a UX student, that made the experience even more valuable.

What was your favourite memory of it?

I loved meeting all kinds of people, from other students to academic advisors. Getting to share my skills and see my designs used for actual events felt really rewarding. We also got free food and prizes after finishing activities. Honestly, that might have been the best part. :)

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Being a first-year student was already a lot, especially as an international student adjusting to a new environment. Sometimes, balancing council tasks with assignments and midterms felt overwhelming. Still, I learned that I didn’t have to figure everything out alone. Speaking up when I needed help meant we could work through things together, and it helped me appreciate the people around me more.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. Things got stressful sometimes, but the people, creative opportunities, and memories made it worth the effort. The free food definitely helped too!

03

Campus ambassador · Communication

Fall 2026

Learning to speak up

What was it that you did?

I worked as a Brantford Recruitment Ambassador, welcoming prospective students and their families. I prepared for campus tours, practised explaining different services, and shared my own experiences as a Laurier student. I also helped with recruitment events.

When did you do it?

In Fall 2026, including recruitment events and tour preparation throughout September and October.

What was good about it?

The job pushed me to practise English and feel more comfortable talking to new people. I also learned to think about what visitors actually wanted to know. That’s something I can bring into UX too: listening to people and explaining things in a way that makes sense to them.

What was your favourite memory of it?

I enjoyed adding my own campus stories to my tour, especially the one about playing music in the Research and Academic Centre. Sharing those little details made it feel more like a conversation and helped me show what student life is like from my own experience.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Remembering all the information while speaking confidently was harder than I expected. During my reverse tour, I got feedback about projecting my voice and explaining some services more clearly. There was a lot to work on, but it helped me understand what to practise next.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. I like meeting visitors and sharing my experiences. I’m still working on my confidence, but I’m getting there!

04

Piano & guitar · Creative practice

During my time at Laurier

A little time for music

What was it that you did?

I spent time playing piano and guitar on campus. Even when classes and work got busy, I tried to make room to practise and enjoy music. It was something creative I could keep coming back to.

When did you do it?

During my time at Laurier, including practice sessions in Room 004 of the Research and Academic Centre.

What was good about it?

Music gave me a break from assignments and a different way to be creative. It also gave me something to share with my parents in Vietnam. I want my future to include time for family and things I enjoy, so keeping music in my routine matters to me.

What was your favourite memory of it?

Recording videos of myself playing and sending them to my parents is my favourite part. It’s a little way to share my life here with them. Also, the acoustics in that room make playing and singing sound so good!

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

It can feel a little awkward when someone walks in while I’m practising. Finding time isn’t always easy either, especially when classes, work, and deadlines all pile up.

Give your experience a letter grade

A — Couldn’t be better. I get to enjoy music and share those moments with my parents. That makes it really special to me.

05

StuDock · Making & UX

October–November 2026

From a sketch to StuDock

What was it that you did?

I helped create StuDock, a desk organizer made from recycled cardboard for our Group Maker Project. I came up with the name and original organizer idea, shared sketches with my group, and worked on building the prototype. I also measured the parts, trimmed the cardboard, and helped put our presentation together.

When did you do it?

During October and November 2026, while we were planning the project and developing our prototype.

What was good about it?

I got to take an idea beyond a drawing and actually build it. We thought about details like shallow compartments, removable dividers, large labels, and contrasting colours to help people find their supplies more easily. It made me think about how someone would actually use the things I design.

What was your favourite memory of it?

Seeing the original sketch become a cardboard prototype was the best part. Having something we could hold, adjust, and prepare for usability testing made the project feel real. It also made discussing changes with my group much easier.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

The measuring and cutting definitely tested my patience. Something could look simple on paper and still need several adjustments before it worked in the prototype. Working on the organizer and presentation at the same time also took a lot of planning.

Give your experience a letter grade

B — For the most part enjoyable. I liked making something useful with my group, even if some of the tiny adjustments took longer than expected.
