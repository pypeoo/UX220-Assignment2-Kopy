My experiences

01

FYLP · Leadership

My first year at Laurier

Getting involved in FYLP

What was it that you did?

I joined the First Year Leadership Program (FYLP) while living in Grand River Hall. I worked with other students, helped with activities, and encouraged people to get involved in campus life. It meant regularly contributing ideas and making time for council responsibilities alongside classes.

When did you do it?

During my first year at Wilfrid Laurier University, while I lived in Grand River Hall.

What was good about it?

I met people outside my classes and felt more connected to campus. I also practised sharing ideas, working with a team, and taking responsibility. These are skills I can use in future design projects.

What was your favourite memory of it?

Receiving recognition for my involvement was a really nice moment. It made me feel that my time and effort mattered and reminded me how much I had gained by getting involved.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Balancing council responsibilities with coursework could be stressful when deadlines came together. I had to learn how to plan my time and be realistic about how much I could take on.

Give your experience a letter grade.

B — For the most part enjoyable. It took effort, but the connections and experience made it worthwhile.

02

Art Hawks · Marketing

September 2026–May 2027

Marketing for Art Hawks

What was it that you did?

I volunteered as Vice President of Marketing for the Art Hawks Council within FYLP. I designed promotional materials, managed social media, and promoted campus events for incoming and current first-year students. Basically, I helped get the word out and encouraged people to join in!

When did you do it?

From September 2026 to May 2027, during my first year at Laurier.

What was good about it?

I built friendships and improved my teamwork, communication, time management, and design skills. I also got to use my creativity outside assignments and make materials that real people would see. As a UX student, that experience was valuable.

What was your favourite memory of it?

I loved meeting different people, from students to academic advisors, and seeing my designs used for actual events. We also got free food and prizes after finishing activities. Honestly, that might have been the best part. :)

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

As an international student adjusting to first year, I sometimes felt overwhelmed balancing council tasks with assignments and midterms. Still, I learned to speak up when I needed help so we could work through things together. It helped me appreciate the people around me more.

Give your experience a letter grade.

B — For the most part enjoyable. It got stressful sometimes, but the people, creativity, and memories made it worth the effort.

03

Campus ambassador · Communication

Fall 2026

Working as a campus ambassador

What was it that you did?

I worked as a Brantford Recruitment Ambassador, welcoming prospective students and their families. I prepared campus tour information, practised explaining services, shared personal experiences, and helped with recruitment events.

When did you do it?

In Fall 2026, including recruitment events and tour preparation in September and October.

What was good about it?

The job pushed me to practise English and become more comfortable talking to new people. I learned to listen to visitors and explain things clearly. Both skills will help me communicate with users and teammates in future UX work.

What was your favourite memory of it?

I enjoyed adding personal stories to my tour, especially about playing music in the Research and Academic Centre. Sharing those details made the tour feel more like a conversation and helped me show campus life from my own perspective.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Remembering every detail while speaking confidently was challenging. Feedback on my reverse tour showed me that I needed to project my voice and explain some services more clearly. Improving took preparation and repeated practice.

Give your experience a letter grade.

B — For the most part enjoyable. I like connecting with visitors, and I’m still building my confidence.

04

Piano & guitar · Music

During my time at Laurier

Making time for music

What was it that you did?

I spent time playing piano and guitar on campus. Even when school and work got busy, I tried to make room to practise. Keeping music in my routine took consistency and gave me something creative to keep coming back to.

When did you do it?

During my time at Laurier, including practice sessions in Room 004 of the Research and Academic Centre.

What was good about it?

Music gave me a break from assignments and another way to be creative. It also gave me something to share with my parents in Vietnam. Making time for music supports my goal of having a balanced life with strong family connections.

What was your favourite memory of it?

My favourite part was recording videos of myself playing and sending them to my parents. It was a little way to share my life here with them. The room’s acoustics also made playing and singing sound so good!

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

It could feel awkward when someone walked in while I was practising. Finding time was also difficult when classes, work, and deadlines started piling up.

Give your experience a letter grade.

A — Couldn’t be better. Enjoying music and sharing those moments with my parents made it special to me.

05

StuDock · Design & Making

October–November 2026

Creating StuDock

What was it that you did?

I helped create StuDock, a recycled cardboard desk organizer for our Group Maker Project. I proposed the name and original idea, shared sketches, built and trimmed the prototype, measured its parts, and helped format our presentation.

When did you do it?

During October and November 2026, while we were planning the project and developing the prototype.

What was good about it?

I got to turn an idea into something physical. We considered shallow compartments, removable dividers, large labels, and contrasting colours to help people find their supplies. It helped me think about how people would actually use something I designed.

What was your favourite memory of it?

Seeing our sketch become a cardboard prototype was the best part. Having something we could hold and adjust made the idea feel real. It also made discussing changes with my group much easier.

What didn’t you enjoy about it?

Something that looked simple on paper could need several adjustments to work in the prototype. Working on the organizer and presentation together also took careful planning.

Give your experience a letter grade.

B — For the most part enjoyable. I liked making something useful with my group, even when the small adjustments took a while.
